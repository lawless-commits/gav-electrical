FAQ

Practical answers

When do I need a Certificate of Compliance (CoC)?

A CoC is required for every electrical installation and must be updated whenever circuits are added or altered. It’s also needed when you sell a property. Conveyancers commonly ask for a CoC issued within the last two years, or one covering any changes made since. We inspect, fix any non-compliances, test and issue the certificate.

Do I need fault-finding or a full rewire?

Most problems (tripping, dead circuits, flickering) are isolated faults that can be traced and repaired. A rewire is usually only recommended when insulation tests fail across many circuits, wiring is very old or unsafe, or the installation can’t support current loads. We test first and show you the results before recommending either.

Will an inverter or battery work with my existing DB and wiring?

Usually yes, but it needs to be integrated properly: a changeover or bypass switch, a separate essential-load DB, correct earthing when running off-grid, and surge protection. Grid-tied or hybrid systems with solar must comply with SANS 10142-1-2 and be registered with your municipality or Eskom where required. We check compatibility during the assessment.

What are typical turnaround times?

Callouts are typically attended the same or next working day, with emergencies such as no power or burning smells prioritised. CoC inspections can usually be booked within a few days. Installation projects are scheduled once the quote is approved and materials are confirmed. Demo timings

Can you work around business hours or estate access rules?

Yes. Commercial work can be scheduled early, after hours or on weekends to avoid disrupting trading. For estates and complexes we work with body corporate and security access requirements. Residential bookings run Monday to Saturday; we’re closed on Sundays except for arranged emergencies.

Is the online estimate a quotation?

No. The estimator gives an indicative starting point based on your bill and chosen loads. A firm, itemised quote follows a site assessment, because DB condition, cable runs, roof space and actual load profiles all affect the final design.
